On a flat-rate plan, a kilowatt-hour costs the same at 5 pm as at 3 am, and this guide can't save you a dime — beyond helping you check whether a time-of-use plan would. But tens of millions of U.S. households are now on **time-of-use (TOU) rates**, where the same load can cost two to three times more at dinnertime than overnight. California put most residents on TOU by default; Arizona, Texas, Colorado, Michigan, and many others offer or default to them.

## How TOU pricing works

Utilities pay wildly different wholesale prices across the day — demand peaks in late afternoon and early evening when everyone gets home, cooks, and cools the house. TOU plans pass that shape on to you, typically as:

- **Peak**: weekday late afternoon/evening, commonly 4–9 pm. The expensive window.
- **Off-peak**: overnight and often midday. The cheap window — sometimes half the peak price or less.
- Some utilities add a **super off-peak** (deep overnight, aimed at EV charging) or seasonal variations.

The gap is the whole game. A plan with 45¢ peak / 25¢ off-peak means a task shifted overnight costs 45% less. Check your utility's exact windows — they vary enormously.

## Which appliances are worth shifting

Shiftability is about two things: does it use a lot of energy per session, and do you care when it runs?

**Prime candidates:**

- **[EV charging](/appliance/ev-charger/)** — the biggest and easiest win. 250–400 kWh/month, fully schedulable from the car or charger. On many TOU plans this alone justifies switching plans.
- **[Dishwasher](/appliance/dishwasher/)** — has a delay-start button; nobody cares when dishes wash. 1–1.5 kWh per load, every day, forever.
- **[Laundry](/appliance/electric-dryer/)** — 3–4 kWh per dryer load. Weekend mornings or after 9 pm.
- **[Electric water heater](/appliance/electric-water-heater/)** — the sleeper hit. A tank stores heat beautifully; a simple timer heating water off-peak can shift 300+ kWh/month without changing a single shower. Some utilities give rebates for controllable water heaters.
- **[Pool pump](/appliance/pool-pump/)** — completely schedulable, and one of the biggest loads at homes that have one.

**Semi-shiftable:**

- **Pre-cooling with AC** — cool the house harder before the peak window, coast through it a few degrees higher. Works best in well-insulated homes.
- **[Oven cooking](/appliance/electric-oven/)** — batch-bake on weekends; use the [air fryer](/appliance/air-fryer/) or microwave during peak.

**Not shiftable, don't bother:** refrigeration, electronics, lighting, and anything tied to when you're actually using it.

## The one-evening setup

1. Find your plan's exact peak window (bill or utility app).
2. Set the EV to charge starting at the off-peak hour — one-time setting.
3. Make delay-start the default dishwasher habit: load after dinner, start at midnight.
4. Put the water heater on a timer if you're handy (or ask about utility programs).
5. Shift laundry to weekends/late evening.

Households that shift EV charging plus laundry, dishes, and hot water routinely move 40–60% of their total usage off-peak — often **$20–60/month** on plans with a wide peak/off-peak spread, for close to zero lifestyle change.

## Should you *choose* a TOU plan?

If you have an EV, almost certainly yes. If you're home all day running AC at 5 pm and can't shift anything, possibly not. Pull your hourly usage data from your utility's portal (most provide it), and compare what last month would have cost on each plan — utilities frequently offer this comparison themselves, and it beats guessing.
