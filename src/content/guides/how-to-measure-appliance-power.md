Every number on this site is a well-founded typical value — but *your* refrigerator, in *your* kitchen, at *your* settings, has one true number. Finding it costs about $10 and takes a day. Here's how.

## The tools

**Smart plug with energy monitoring ($8–15).** The modern default. Plug it in, put the device on it, read the app: instantaneous watts, cumulative kWh, often per-day history. Kasa, Tapo, and similar brands are accurate within a few percent — plenty for bill math. Bonus: it's also a schedule and a remote switch, so the meter *is* the fix.

**Kill A Watt-style plug meter ($20–35).** The classic standalone: no app or account, shows watts/kWh on a screen. Fine choice if you'd rather not put your lamp on Wi-Fi.

**Whole-house options.** Many utility portals already show hourly usage — free and revealing (look for the overnight baseline). Panel-level monitors (Emporia, Sense, $100–300) itemize everything at once; overkill for most, wonderful for data lovers.

**What you can't plug-meter:** hardwired 240 V loads — [central AC](/appliance/central-air-conditioner/), [water heaters](/appliance/electric-water-heater/), [electric furnaces](/appliance/electric-furnace/), [EV chargers](/appliance/ev-charger/). Those need the utility-portal method (compare days with and without the load), a panel monitor, or our state-adjusted estimates.

## How to measure the two kinds of device

**Steady or always-on devices** ([router](/appliance/wifi-router/), [aquarium](/appliance/aquarium/), fridge, media center): don't trust a 10-second reading — compressors and duty cycles mislead. Leave the meter on for **24–48 hours** and read cumulative kWh. Divide by days, multiply by 30.4 and your rate: that's the monthly bill line. (Rate = your bill's total ÷ kWh, or [your state's average](/states/).)

**Session devices** ([dryer](/appliance/electric-dryer/), [dishwasher](/appliance/dishwasher/), [air fryer](/appliance/air-fryer/), [treadmill](/appliance/treadmill/)): reset the meter, run one full normal session, read kWh per session. Multiply by sessions per week × 4.35 for monthly cost.

## What to measure first

In rough order of revelation-per-effort:

1. **The refrigerator** — the biggest measurable-by-plug load, and the one where age matters most. A modern unit reads 1–1.5 kWh/day; an old or dying one can read 3–5. This single measurement can justify a replacement.
2. **The entertainment center, overnight** — everything "off," measure 8 idle hours. This is your [phantom-load](/guides/phantom-load-standby-power/) reality check.
3. **The gaming PC or home office cluster** — people guess these wildly wrong in both directions.
4. **One dryer load and one dishwasher load** — your per-session truth beats any table.
5. **Anything you argue about** — the meter settles marital energy disputes instantly.

## Reading the results without fooling yourself

- **Watch for duty cycle.** A [space heater](/appliance/space-heater/) "on high" cycles its element with the thermostat; peak watts ≠ average watts. Cumulative kWh over hours is the honest number.
- **Standby counts.** A device used 1 hour a day at 100 W but idling at 10 W spends more than half its energy in standby. The 24-hour measurement captures this; spot checks don't.
- **Seasons change answers.** The garage fridge in July ≠ January. The dehumidifier's duty cycle tracks humidity. Measure in the season you're worried about.
- **Compare against expectations.** Our [appliance pages](/appliances/) give the typical range — if your device reads far above it, you've found either a settings problem, a failing appliance, or a genuine surprise worth fixing.

One weekend of measuring the top five suspects turns your electric bill from a mystery novel into a spreadsheet — and the plugs you bought to do it become the timers that fix what you find.
