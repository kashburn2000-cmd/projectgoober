In hot-climate states, cooling can be more than half of a summer electric bill. The good news: cooling costs respond to strategy more than almost any other load — the same comfort can cost wildly different amounts depending on how you buy it.

## What each option costs per hour

At the U.S. average rate (~17¢/kWh), typical costs while running:

| Cooling option | Draw | Cost/hour |
|---|---|---|
| [Ceiling fan](/appliance/ceiling-fan/) | 15–90 W | 0.3–1.5¢ |
| [Box/tower fan](/appliance/box-fan/) | 40–100 W | 0.7–1.7¢ |
| [Evaporative cooler](/appliance/evaporative-cooler/) (dry climates) | 100–800 W | 2–14¢ |
| [Window AC](/appliance/window-air-conditioner/) | 500–1,500 W | 9–26¢ |
| [Mini-split](/appliance/mini-split/) (one zone) | 400–2,000 W | 7–34¢ |
| [Portable AC](/appliance/portable-air-conditioner/) | 700–1,500 W | 12–26¢ |
| [Central AC](/appliance/central-air-conditioner/) | 2,000–5,000 W | 34–85¢ |

Multiply by your state's ratio — [California roughly doubles these](/states/), the cheapest states cut them by a third. Two structural facts jump out: **fans are two orders of magnitude cheaper than compressors**, and **cooling one room is far cheaper than cooling all of them**.

## The five highest-leverage moves

**1. Raise the setpoint, add air movement.** Each degree higher saves roughly 2–3% of cooling energy — and moving air feels 3–4°F cooler than still air. 72°F with still air and 76°F with ceiling fans feel similar and cost meaningfully different amounts. (Turn fans off in empty rooms; they cool skin, not spaces.)

**2. Cool the room you're in, not the house you own.** A [window unit](/appliance/window-air-conditioner/) or [mini-split](/appliance/mini-split/) serving the bedroom or office at full comfort, with central AC set several degrees warmer (or off), is the biggest structural saving available — the strategy, with numbers, is in [our central-vs-window comparison](/compare/central-air-conditioner-vs-window-air-conditioner/).

**3. Block heat before it enters.** Solar gain through windows is a huge fraction of cooling load. Closed blinds/curtains on sun-facing windows during the day, exterior shading where possible, and closed windows and doors once outdoor temps exceed indoor. Every blocked BTU is cooling you don't buy.

**4. Maintain the machine.** Dirty filters and a fouled outdoor coil force longer runtimes for the same cooling — an easy 10–25% penalty. Filters monthly in season; hose the outdoor unit gently each spring. If the system is pre-2010, its SEER rating alone may justify replacement — [the label math](/guides/energy-star-labels-explained/).

**5. Exploit cool nights.** Where nights drop below ~70°F, flushing the house with night air ([whole-house fan](/appliance/whole-house-fan/) or strategic window fans) and closing up at dawn can eliminate morning AC hours entirely — dry Western climates are built for this.

## Traps that raise summer bills

- **The [portable AC](/appliance/portable-air-conditioner/) as a permanent solution** — single-hose designs fight themselves; a window unit does the same job for 30–70% less energy.
- **Closing vents to "push" cooling elsewhere** — raises duct pressure, often increases total energy, stresses the blower.
- **Big thermostat swings on conventional AC** — "off all day, blast at 5 pm" can cost more than a modest setback, and lands the load exactly at [peak TOU pricing](/guides/cheapest-time-to-run-appliances/). Pre-cooling before the peak window is the TOU-aware version that works.
- **The [dehumidifier](/appliance/dehumidifier/) + AC double-pay** — in humid climates a basement dehumidifier is often necessary, but set it to 50%, not "continuous," or it becomes a second compressor running all summer.

## The seasonal reality check

Run the [bill analyzer](/) with your cooling setup and state — seeing that central AC is $80–250/month of your summer bill while fans are $3 reframes every other decision. Cooling is the one bill line where comfort and cost aren't actually in conflict: the cheap tools (fans, shade, night air) *add* comfort per dollar rather than trading it away.
