Few pieces of energy advice are repeated more than "unplug things to save money" — and few are repeated with less arithmetic. Let's do the arithmetic.

## What "off" actually draws

When a device is off but plugged in, it may draw standby power for remote receivers, clocks, network connections, or simply a cheap power supply that trickles. The scale:

| Device class | Standby draw | Annual cost (US avg) |
|---|---|---|
| Modern phone/laptop chargers (empty) | 0.05–0.3 W | $0.10–0.50 |
| Toaster, kettle, most "dumb" appliances | ~0 W | ~$0 |
| Microwave / coffee maker (clock) | 1–4 W | $1.50–6 |
| TV (modern) | 0.5–3 W | $1–4.50 |
| Soundbar / speakers | 1–5 W | $1.50–7.50 |
| [Game console](/appliance/game-console/), instant-on | 10–15 W | $15–22 |
| [Cable/satellite DVR](/appliance/cable-box/) | 15–25 W | $22–37 |
| Older AV receivers / subwoofers | 5–15 W | $7.50–22 |

The pattern: **unplugging most things saves approximately nothing**. The classic targets of unplugging folklore — toasters, phone chargers, lamps — cost pennies per year plugged in. The real money hides in a handful of media devices that maintain network connections and instant-wake states.

## The five unplugs (or settings) that actually pay

1. **The DVR you no longer use.** $20–35/yr, more in high-rate states. If you've moved to streaming, it's the single best unplug in the house.
2. **Console instant-on → energy saver.** Not an unplug — a menu setting. $15–20/yr per console, and everything still works.
3. **The entertainment-center cluster on one switchable strip.** TV + receiver + soundbar + accessories, killed together at night: $20–50/yr for a well-stocked setup.
4. **The guest room / rarely-used TV setup.** Devices that idle 8,760 hours to be used for 20.
5. **Anything with a warm power brick while "off."** Warmth is watts. Measure it, then decide — [here's how](/guides/how-to-measure-appliance-power/).

Total realistic ceiling for a diligent household: **$50–150/yr**, mostly from the media cluster — double that in [California or New England rates](/states/). Worth one evening of setup? Absolutely. Worth unplugging the toaster daily for 30 years? That's a lifestyle, not a savings plan.

## Where unplugging folklore goes wrong

- **"Chargers burn money all night."** A modern charger with nothing attached draws well under a watt — under a dollar a year. With the phone attached, you're paying for charging, which was the point.
- **"Unplug the microwave."** Its clock costs ~$3/yr. If re-setting the clock costs you ten seconds twice a week, you're working for well under minimum wage.
- **"The fridge at night…"** No. Refrigeration is the one load that must run continuously; unplugging it costs you groceries.
- **What people miss while unplugging toasters:** the [always-on 100+ watt floor](/guides/phantom-load-standby-power/) of routers, cameras, servers, and set-top boxes — and the four-figure loads of [heating](/guides/space-heater-cost-mistakes/), cooling, and [hot water](/appliance/electric-water-heater/) that dwarf everything in this article.

## The rational policy

Do the five real unplugs once, with strips and settings so they maintain themselves. Then redirect the unplugging energy toward the thermostat, the water heater setting, and [whatever tops your own bill breakdown](/) — where the same attention is worth ten to fifty times more.
